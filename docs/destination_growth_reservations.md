## 1. Status

This document describes a proposed local destination growth reservation protocol. It is not implemented or qualified. Current gateway capacity checks sample available space for a single operation; they do not reserve destination growth durably across resources. The staging quota ledger does not account for final repository growth. Enabled jobs must not rely on the current checks as a cross-resource free-space guarantee.

## 2. Accounting and authority

Final repository bytes and temporary staging bytes have different lifetimes. Keep a durable destination growth ledger separate from staging quota: final backup data is not charged to the 1 GiB local spool limit. A destination entry reserves an upper bound on additional physical writes for one initialization or publication execution, including its bounded repository write budget. Persist the operation domain and identity, resource and root identity, canonical volume identity, epoch and fence, immutable byte limit, and held or released state. Publication entries also bind the admission and execution ordinal. Bound the number of records and use checked integer arithmetic. Replays with changed bindings or limits fail before effects; a later physical execution needs its own reservation.

All cooperating local stores on a volume use one canonical database authority and a common node/volume coordinator. Derive the volume identity from the pinned endpoint's actual device and verify the root binding. An independently operated database or uncooperative writer is outside this authority and must not be treated as covered by the ledger. Preserve the existing physical-root and mount-identity checks; an identity mismatch or unavailable capacity observation fails closed.

Under that coordinator, sample capacity and available bytes from the pinned volume, then use a short immediate database transaction to check and reserve capacity. The checked admission condition is available bytes at least the sum of held destination growth reservations, charged staging reservations on the same volume, the requested growth, and the greater of 512 MiB or five percent of capacity rounded up. Apply the same common coordinator and combined accounting to staging admission, replay, inventory changes, and any future release. Sampling must occur after acquiring the coordinator, and releases must use it as well: otherwise an old free-space sample can outlive a concurrent worker's writes and reservation release. Counting a full outstanding reservation against available space can count already-written bytes twice; this is conservative and may reject work.

The coordinator covers the capacity observation and short journal transaction, not the entire worker lifetime. Each outstanding reservation remains durable while the worker runs. Sampling and filesystem checks must not occur inside the SQLite write transaction. Filesystem capacity calls can block; isolating and bounding those calls remains a separate production requirement.

## 3. Operation and recovery

Initialization and publication must persist their growth reservation in the same database transaction as the corresponding transition to a state that permits a mutating worker to launch. The recorded worker-scope callback must still bind the actual kernel scope before fork. A failed reservation or failed journal transition permits no destination mutation. An initialization or copy worker's own write budget remains independently enforced.

Retain the full reservation through a timeout, crash, unresolved effect, failed verification, or unknown worker state. A crash after native writes and before journaling completion remains charged. Release is permitted only after the recorded mutating scope is proved quiescent and the outcome is durably verified or an authenticated negative census establishes the operation's effect is absent. Release and the qualifying journal transition must be atomic under the same volume coordinator. An unsuccessful inspection does not prove absence or release capacity. A pending worker retains both its kernel-scope protection and its reservation; releasing a process-local permit alone is insufficient. Initialization recovery must also respect its one-attempt journal and must not reinitialize an unknown repository.

A schema migration needs a closed inventory gate when existing potentially mutating initialization or publication records have no corresponding growth reservation. A trusted recovery procedure must either reconstruct conservative holds or prove each old mutator quiescent and its outcome settled before opening that gate. Missing scope information is not proof that a worker is absent. Inventory completion, replay, and restart must preserve outstanding charges; no automatic deletion or unsafe reclamation is implied.

## 4. Integration points

Replace the standalone `LocalGatewayStore::check_capacity` admission with coordinated growth reservation for initialization and publication. Couple reservation with `StateStore::start_gateway_initialization` and `StateStore::start_gateway_execution`, rather than placing a separate reservation call beside either transition. Couple release with the relevant initialization completion, `transition_gateway_execution` negative proof, or `record_gateway_receipt` verification transaction after quiescence. The existing `reserve_scratch`, `reserve_owned_staging_quota`, and `StateStore::reserve_staging_quota` paths must participate in the same volume coordination and combined capacity calculation. Their existing scratch and staging limits remain in force.

The exact schema and APIs remain implementation work. Trusted callers must supply evidence from pinned endpoints and recorded worker scopes; a journal flag alone is not physical verification. Source lifetime protection, recovery on other platforms, independent databases, external filesystem writers, and remote destinations remain separate gates.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

- Concurrent admissions from separate database handles for independent resources on one volume cannot jointly exceed the available capacity and reserve; different volumes are independently accounted. Staging and gateway admissions competing for the same volume obey the same bound.
- A synchronized release/admission test proves that a capacity sample taken before completed writes cannot be reused after their reservation is released. Retained final bytes reduce a subsequent fresh sample.
- Exact replay preserves a held charge. Changed operation, root, volume, fence, or byte budget is rejected without effects. Overflow, record limits, insufficient space, a missing or changed mount, and an incomplete legacy inventory fail closed.
- Restart, worker timeout, unknown outcome, and failed or ambiguous verification retain the charge. A late or unquiesced worker cannot race release. Verified completion and authenticated negative proof release exactly once with the corresponding journal transition; interruption before that transition retains the charge.
- Migration fixtures cover legacy active and unknown operations, inventory reconstruction, and refusal to open admission while any old effect lacks a conservative hold or adequate proof. Tests verify that final backup bytes are not added to the staging quota and that native writes do not begin on failed admission.
- Native tests demonstrate the protocol under the supported local filesystem, process containment, and single-authority assumptions. These tests do not establish guarantees against external writers, cloned authorities, remote storage, or unbounded metadata calls.

## 6. Capacity Domain Qualification

The proposed `unix:<device>` identity in the initial candidate is insufficient for Btrfs free-space accounting. Linux Btrfs explicitly mixes the subvolume root ID into `f_fsid`, so neither `st_dev` nor generic `fstatvfs().f_fsid` establishes a common pool across subvolumes. The implementation needs the full filesystem ID from `BTRFS_IOC_FS_INFO` on a suitable pinned descriptor, with unsupported or ambiguous observations rejected. This is verified from pinned source, rather than a mounted Btrfs qualification test. See [Btrfs statfs](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.12/fs/btrfs/super.c), [filesystem information ioctl](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.12/fs/btrfs/ioctl.c), and [glibc statvfs conversion](https://github.com/bminor/glibc/blob/glibc-2.40/sysdeps/unix/sysv/linux/internal_statvfs.c).

Existing staging device identities need a verified mapping to capacity domains. Unknown, removed, or replaced roots cannot be assigned a domain from their historical device number. Project quotas add narrower limits, and arbitrary shared thin backing pools are not proved independent by filesystem identifiers. The first safe implementation may conservatively group unresolved domains rather than assume independence. Real mounted subvolume, alias, project-quota, and legacy inventory tests remain acceptance gates. The candidate under `build/next-gate` is deliberately unwired and has not established this mapping.
