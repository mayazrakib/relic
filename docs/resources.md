## 1. Resource Admission Foundations

The resource pool accounts for explicit reservations against global, job, and endpoint caps in one nonblocking transaction. A rejected reservation changes no counters. Dropping a reservation releases its charge. An endpoint-tagged data reservation is limited to 16 MiB, and endpoint stream, worker, file, cache, and spool limits leave capacity for other endpoints when the configured global limit permits it. The low and balanced defaults follow the resource classes in the specification. Additional caps only lower those defaults.

A separate 32 MiB control reservation is unavailable to data reservations. Shared buffers retain a single charge until the last clone is dropped. Allocation is fallible and limited to 8 MiB per buffer. Cancellation and monotonic deadlines are checked before admission; stage implementations must also check them during work. Admission does not wait, so cancellation does not need to wake an admission queue.

Accounting only covers resources that callers reserve and report. Callers must charge shared buffers to their owner, attach the correct endpoint to endpoint work, acquire permits before allocating or opening resources, and retain them for the complete resource lifetime. The pool does not measure resident memory, kernel buffers, filesystem free space, external engine memory, child processes, or other uninstrumented allocations. It does not yet enforce directory frontier limits, endpoint fairness over time, scheduling, cache reclamation, or power constraints. Spool admission does not replace the required free-space reserve or spool encryption. Control reservations do not allocate or guarantee memory.

A process or container needs an external operating-system memory limit, such as a cgroup, for a hard resident-memory bound. Even that limit is a final containment mechanism and does not replace in-process admission and accounting.

`jobs` is an explicit counter, not a count inferred from distinct job identifiers. Each admitted active job requires one job reservation, held until all its work has ended. Other job and endpoint reservations must remain inside that lifetime. Endpoint quotas apply only to requests tagged with the endpoint. A nonblocking admission can briefly contend for the accounting mutex; it never waits for resource capacity or retries automatically. Cancellation and a deadline are sampled at the admission boundary, so a stage must check again before effects and throughout its work. Endpoint caps cannot reserve capacity for another endpoint when the global cap itself is one unit, and they do not guarantee scheduling fairness.

`ResourcePool::admit_job` creates an `ActiveJob` holding one job permit. `ActiveJob::reserve_stage` creates a stage reservation that retains the job lease, so dropping the original handle cannot release the job while any stage remains active. Stage requests must not include a job count. Existing direct reservations remain available for compatibility and do not automatically retain an active-job lease; callers using them must maintain that lifetime themselves.
